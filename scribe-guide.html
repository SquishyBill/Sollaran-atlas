Keeping the Session Journal

For the player who keeps the party’s record: how to write each session into the atlas, bring in your old summaries, and keep the chronicle tidy.

Written for the Scribe · Ink not included
Part I

Your Role

The party has chosen you to keep its chronicle. Each session you write a summary, and it appears in the atlas as an entry in the Session Journal, where everyone can read it, find it by date, and add comments of their own. It replaces the shared document, and it lives beside the maps, people and shops it talks about.

Who sees what

The partyReads every published entry, and may comment on it. They cannot change your words.
YouWrite, edit, publish and delete entries. You also see your own drafts.
The DMCan do everything you can, and decides who is the scribe.

Before you begin

  1. Sign in to the atlas with the Google address your DM gave the atlas for you.
  2. Open the journal with the open-book icon in the bar at the top.
  3. You should see a red + New entry button. If you do not, tell your DM which Google address you use; they add it in the journal panel under Who may write the journal.

Writing your first entry

  1. Press + New entry.
  2. Check the session number (the atlas suggests the next one) and the date played. The date is what players see on each folded entry, so make it the day you played.
  3. Give it a title that will mean something later, such as The Night at the Roosting Crow.
  4. Write the summary in the big box. The next page shows the little formatting you can use.
  5. Press Preview to see how it will look, then Write to go back to the text.
  6. Press Save as a draft to keep it private for now, or Publish to show it to the party.

You cannot break anything. A draft is seen only by you and the DM, and you can change or delete any entry later.

Part II

Writing and Formatting

Plain sentences are all you need. When you want a little more, the journal understands a few simple marks. Type the left column, and the right is what the party sees.

You typeThe party sees
**Sister Eventide**Sister Eventide in bold
*the Night Scale*the Night Scale in italics
# The CrossroadsA heading (put it on its own line, with a blank line before it)
- Bought three rationsA bullet point (start each line with a dash and a space)
> “Trust no one.”A quotation
[[Emmett]]A gold link to Emmett that opens its page or entry
[[Emmett|the village]]A link that shows the words the village
[words](https://…)A link to a web page

Links to the atlas are the journal’s best trick: a name in double square brackets becomes a link to that place, person or shop, as long as the name matches what the atlas calls it. A name the atlas does not know shows up greyed, so you can see when something is misspelled.

Bringing in what you have already written

  1. Open a summary in your Google Doc, select the session you want, and copy it (Ctrl+C).
  2. Press + New entry and click in the big box.
  3. Paste (Ctrl+V). Bold, italics, headings, lists and links come across and are turned into the marks above. A short message tells you it worked.
  4. Set the session number and the date, add the title, and read it through with Preview.
  5. Save it as a draft, and publish when you are happy. Do each session as its own entry.

Pictures and fancy tables do not come across, and a table turns into plain lines. If you would rather have no formatting at all, paste with Ctrl+Shift+V instead. Entries always appear in order of session number, whatever order you add them in, so you may do the oldest ones last.

Part III

Drafts, Edits and Good Habits

Drafts and publishing

  • Save as a draft keeps the entry to yourself and the DM. It shows a small draft tag, and the party cannot see it or find it in a search.
  • Publish makes it visible to the whole party at once.
  • On a published entry, Save changes updates it for everyone straight away. Make it a draft again takes it back out of sight while you fix something.
  • Delete is for good, so be sure.

Finding and reading entries

Entries are listed newest first and folded up, each showing its date and title, so the party can scan for a session. Press one to open it. Entries also turn up in the atlas search.

Comments from the party

Under each entry the other players can leave party notes: “I think we walked for two days”, “She was called Mara, not Mira.” Their notes sit beneath your entry and never change it; fix the entry yourself if they are right.

A shape that works (a couple of minutes to read)

  • Where we started, and where we ended.
  • What happened, in the order it happened.
  • Who we met, as links to their entries, so the names are easy to find again.
  • What we decided, bought or found.
  • Loose ends: questions, promises, debts and rumors still open.
